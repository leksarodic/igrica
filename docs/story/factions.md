# Factions

*Carrow Vale, year 612. No faction is evil. Every faction is partly right, and every faction has done something it does not discuss.*

---

## The Tenders

**What they are.** The valley's ritual and archival class. They keep the Long Bell, the Tending calendar, the listening jars, the tower, and the careful language that makes all of it sound like maintenance.

**Structure.** A Warden leads, advised by a council of three: the Bell-Keeper, the Archivist, and the Spring-Warden. Below them are the tower keepers, the lantern crews, and the record-keepers. It is a small organization with an outsized moral weight. In the game, Sable Voss is Warden emerita; the council governs, and she is consulted when they are frightened, which is often.

**Belief.** Continuity is the only thing standing between the valley and silence. The Note held for four hundred years because someone was willing to do the unpleasant arithmetic. The fact that the arithmetic is hidden is not a lie; it is a kindness. People cannot carry what they do not know.

**Goal.** Keep the valley stable. Rebuild the Chimeworks if possible. Keep the Quarry Door sealed. Find a way to pay the Note's debt that does not require another child.

**Resources.** The tower, the archive, the jars, the calendar, the town's trust, and the only complete record of the Cantors' procedures.

**Internal tensions.** The old guard (led by **Bell-Keeper Vess**) believes the Tending must continue in secret. The reformers (led by a young archivist, **Iset**) believe the town must be told everything and taught the Chord properly. Sable sits between them, too tired to lead and too guilty to retire. The player's choices can strengthen either wing.

**Relationship with the player.** Wary respect. They will help with lore, rituals, and access to the tower, but they will not hand over the jars. If the player exposes the Harvests publicly, the Tenders fracture; if the player works with them quietly, they reform slowly. Neither path is "correct."

**Ending preference.** The Note. Stability, continuity, a managed debt, a secret kept by people who believe they are protecting everyone else.

## The Quarry Guild

**What they are.** The valley's miners, cutters, masons, and builders. Blunt, proud, dusty, and indispensable. They keep the roads, the roofs, the bridges, and the deep shaft.

**Structure.** A foreman leads, elected by the crews. **Maud Kettle** has held the post for six years. Her father Brannoc is the guild's living history and its most annoying retired member. Crews are organized by shaft and trade: cutters, haulers, finishers, and the small, unlucky team that works the deep humstone seam.

**Belief.** The valley should pay for its own upkeep. Secrecy is a luxury of people who have never had a roof fall on them. The Tenders have run Carrow Vale like a locked room for four centuries, and the room is falling down. Open the door, study what is behind it, and make it useful.

**Goal.** Reopen the deep quarry, secure the humstone supply, and force the Tenders to share what they know. Maud also wants, though she would not phrase it this way, for her father to admit that the Guild was right.

**Resources.** The quarry, the roads, the foundry, the labour, and the deep shaft where the Quarry Door was found.

**Internal tensions.** The old crews remember the cave-ins and the singing stone; they want to go slowly. The younger crews want the humstone and the contracts. Brannoc argues for caution and then undermines his own argument by being unable to resist a problem.

**Relationship with the player.** Open, transactional, and warm once trust is earned. They will sell materials, upgrade equipment, and provide access to the Quarry Door. They resent being treated as muscle. If the player closes the door without consulting them, they remember it.

**Ending preference.** The Chord, in the sense of a rebuilt, shared Chimeworks — but with the Guild's hands on the stone and the Guild's name on the works. They are not romantics. They want the valley to work.

## The Reedfolk

**What they are.** Boat families who move between Reedwater and the Low Water. They cut reed, carry freight, fish, and keep the channels open. They are the valley's oldest continuous culture and its least institutional.

**Structure.** No single leader. Families are organized around boats and houseboats; elders are consulted, not obeyed. **Nissa Quill** speaks for the Reedwater families in the game, partly because she is good at it and partly because no one else wants the meetings. **Orrin Quill**, her father, is the boatwright and the quiet centre of the family.

**Belief.** The Hush is a tide, not an enemy. The valley has always changed; the Cantors tried to stop it and made a debt. You do not fight a tide. You learn when to move, when to moor, and when to let something go. The dead are put in the reeds, not the bell, because the reeds know how to return things.

**Goal.** Keep the channels open, keep the families moving, and stop the Tenders from making another child into a foundation. They want the Returning treated as neighbours, not as a security problem.

**Resources.** Boats, channels, reed beds, knowledge of the Low Water, and the only practical map of the Hush's seasonal movement.

**Internal tensions.** Some families want to leave the valley entirely and settle in the Low Water. Others, including Nissa, want to stay and change the town from within. The elders tolerate the Tenders; the young do not.

**Relationship with the player.** Hospitable but watchful. They will feed you before they trust you. If the player helps the ferry and respects the reed rites, they open the Low Water routes and the Hush paths. If the player treats them as scenery, they become politely unavailable.

**Ending preference.** The Silence. Let the Hush pass, let the valley change, and build a culture that moves with it instead of a wall that fails.

## The Listeners

**What they are.** Not an organization. A scattered inheritance of people who can hear the undertone. They appear in every faction and none. Most never meet another Listener.

**Tradition.** A notebook, a handshake (two fingers to the ear, then the ground), and three rules:

1. Never answer a voice you cannot see.
2. Write down what you hear before you decide what it means.
3. Do not take a voice, even to save a valley.

The rules are not magical. They are the accumulated common sense of people who have watched other Listeners make mistakes.

**Resources.** Notebooks, rope maps, tuning forks, and the ability to hear resonance that others cannot. The player is one of them.

**Internal tensions.** Mira Alk broke the third rule in a way that saved the valley and cost her everything. Wren Alk kept it and lost her brother. The player inherits the argument.

**Relationship with the player.** Wren is the only living Listener in the valley. She is glad not to be alone and frightened of what the player might do. The game never makes the player join anything.

**Ending preference.** None. The Listeners are a way of paying attention, not a program for the valley.

## The Choir (antagonistic force)

**What it is.** What remains of the Cantors' practice after the Long Silence: a collection of voices, jars, and procedures that continues to operate without a living conductor. After the novella, the Choirmaster is dispersed, but the Vault is still full, and something in it is still arranging.

**In the game.** The Choir manifests as **the Returning** (voices that have partially come home and do not know what to do), the **Keeper of the Quarry Door** (a Cantor guardian that mistakes the Guild for a maintenance crew), and, late, a new **Choirmaster** — not Ilse Vey, but a composite of released voices trying to make a shape that can hold them. It is not evil. It is a institution without a conscience, still running its procedures.

**Goal.** Complete the choir. Preserve every voice. It does not understand that preservation can be a prison, because it was built by people who did not understand it either.

**Resources.** The Vault, the jars, the Undersong, the Chimeworks' original tuning floor, and four hundred years of procedure.

**Relationship with the player.** It wants the player to join, conduct, or replace it. It offers genuine gifts: lost voices, lost memories, a chance to hear the dead. It never lies outright. It simply does not understand "no."

**Ending role.** In the Chord ending, the Choir is disbanded and its voices returned. In the Note ending, it is reabsorbed into a new, voluntary Note. In the Silence ending, it is left to dissolve as the Hush passes. None of these is a simple victory.

## Institutions and civic life

- **The Listening House** — archive, school, and public reading room. Run by Pell and a rotating staff of two. The closest thing the valley has to a university.
- **The ferry** — the north channel crossing, run by the Quill family. A civic service, not a business; the fare is a suggestion.
- **The market** — three permanent stalls and a weekly barter day. No currency obsession; marks are copper tokens, useful but not sacred.
- **The tower** — the Hollow Bell, the Long Bell, the Tenders' seat. Public on festival days, sealed otherwise.
- **The quarry** — the Guild's workplace, the valley's economic engine, and the source of the current crisis.

## Reputation and consequences

The game tracks trust with the Tenders, the Quarry Guild, and the Reedfolk on a 0–5 scale. Trust changes prices, dialogue, side work, and epilogue details. **It never gates the main story.** The player can finish the game with every faction disappointed in them.

Major choices and their visible consequences:

| Choice | Tenders | Guild | Reedfolk | World |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Give the Choir Chime to the Tenders | + | − | − | North channel runs clear; town lanterns relit; Reedfolk lose the winter crossing |
| Give the Chime to the Reedfolk | − | 0 | + | Channels reopen wild; ferry thrives; town market thins |
| Install the Chime in the player's camp | − | − | − | Player gains a powerful ability; both factions feel used; a third option opens later |
| Expose the Harvests publicly | − | + | + | Tenders fracture; reform possible; town trust shaken |
| Work with Sable quietly | + | − | 0 | Slower reform; Sable's guilt deepens; a secret ending variant |
| Open the Quarry Door with Maud | − | + | 0 | Humstone supply; the Keeper wakes; a new dungeon |
| Seal the Quarry Door | + | − | + | Stability; Guild radicalizes; a strike subplot |
| Let the Returning stay | − | 0 | + | Colour returns to the Hush edge; a new festival; Tenders alarmed |

The world reacts in small, legible ways: a repaired ferry, a new stall, a closed shutter, a child's song, a faction banner, a line of dialogue from a character who remembers.
