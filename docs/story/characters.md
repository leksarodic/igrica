# Characters

*Spoilers for the novella. Ages are given for the novella (year 600) and the game (year 612).*

---

## Wren Alk

**Born** 583. **Novella** 17. **Game** 29. **Role** Listener of Carrow Vale; mentor and quest-giver in the game.

Wren is narrow and weathered, with a reed-cutter's hook at her belt and a coat the colour of wet slate. She counts things under her breath. She makes jokes at the worst moments and then looks surprised when people laugh. She left the valley at eleven, a year after her mother walked into the Hush, and spent six years in the Low Water being looked after by an aunt she did not much like. She came back at seventeen to bury her father and stayed.

Her defining wound is not that she lost her brother. It is that she let go of his hand in the fog and has never told anyone. Her defining strength is that she keeps listening anyway.

**Voice:** dry, concrete, allergic to sentiment. She asks questions instead of answering them. When she is frightened she becomes very precise about small physical things. She does not say "I love you"; she says "put me in the reeds."

**Arc (novella):** from a returned stranger who wants to bury her father and leave, to a woman who chooses to stay and teach a town to sing badly. She does not stop grieving. She stops letting grief make her choices.

**Game role:** the player's first mentor in Bellhollow. She is glad someone else can hear the undertone and guilty about it. She will not solve the player's puzzles, but she will tell them what she knows, including the parts that make her look bad. She is the one who wrote the letter in the Listening House.

**Secret:** she still hears Tam in the cracked bell on windless nights and has never decided whether it is memory or an echo.

**Dialogue samples:**

> "I'm not a Listener. I'm a woman who can't stop hearing. There's a difference, and it's mostly paperwork."

> "The valley doesn't need a hero. It needs about forty people who can carry a tune and are willing to look foolish in public."

> "You want the truth? The truth is a door. Most people stand in front of it and admire the hinges."

## Sable Voss

**Born** 542. **Novella** 58. **Game** 70. **Role** Warden of the Note (novella); Tender elder and keeper of the tower (game).

Tall, straight-backed, grey hair pulled tight. She wears a slate coat with copper bells down the spine and a copper ear plate worked with holes. After the novella she loses her hearing in the release and wears the plate no longer. She learns to read lips and writes on a slate.

Sable is not a monster and not a martyr. She is a woman who was handed an impossible job at thirty and did it for thirty years, making the smallest choice available each time, and never letting herself add up the total. She lost her husband, Elias, to the Hush two years before the novella. She has been holding the door shut ever since.

**Voice:** formal, precise, avoids contractions when she is angry or afraid. She uses "we" for the town and "I" only when she is taking responsibility. She does not raise her voice. She asks questions she already knows the answer to, as a courtesy.

**Arc (novella):** from the keeper of the arithmetic to the woman who breaks it. She is not redeemed; she is changed. She rings the giving stroke knowing it may kill the valley and will certainly deafen her. She lives with the consequences.

**Game role:** advisor, obstacle, and the only person who remembers the Cantors' procedures. She believes the Quarry Door must stay sealed. She is right about the danger and wrong about the reason. She and Wren have made a fragile peace; she calls the player "Listener" and means it as both respect and warning.

**Secret:** she kept a private jar of her husband's voice and has never fed it to anything. It is in a drawer under her slate books.

**Dialogue samples:**

> "You are asking me to be a liar. I have been a liar for thirty years. I am good at it. That is not the same as being wrong."

> "The law is old. Old is not the same as wise. It is, however, the same as difficult to argue with at dawn."

> "I do not need forgiveness. I need someone to do the arithmetic honestly. You may start."

## Nissa Quill

**Born** 581. **Novella** 19. **Game** 31. **Role** Reedfolk ferry keeper, reed-reader; companion in the game.

Nissa is compact and quick, brown as a reed stem, with river-glass beads in her hair and a sling of woven reed. She talks with her hands. She maps sound with knots in a cord, a Reedfolk habit she learned from her mother. She is brave in the ordinary way: she is frightened and does the thing anyway, and then makes a joke about it.

Her mother, **Perrin**, chose to walk into the Hush when her illness became unbearable, as some Reedfolk do. Nissa does not think of this as suicide; she thinks of it as a tide. Her father, **Orrin**, a boatwright, is still alive and still pretends he is not worried about her.

**Voice:** quick, warm, teasing, uses river metaphors without noticing. She calls Wren "Alk" when she is annoyed and "Wren" when she is serious. She swears creatively and never cruelly.

**Arc:** from a young woman who helps because someone has to, to a leader who chooses to stay at the ferry rather than become a hero. She is not a sidekick. She has her own stake in every faction choice and says so.

**Game role:** first companion, ranged specialist, and the player's introduction to Reedfolk ethics. Ability **True Pitch** reveals an enemy's pitch and hums the reference note for the Chime Gate. She comments on the world, argues with Sable, and refuses to solve puzzles for the player. She is a friend, not a romance option; the game is explicit about this and does not tease otherwise.

**Secret:** she has heard her mother's voice in the Returning and has not told anyone, because she is afraid it means the Hush is not a tide but a trap.

**Dialogue samples:**

> "Ferry's closed. Also the sky is falling, but the ferry's the more immediate problem."

> "You can tell a lot about a person by how they hold a pole. You're holding it like it owes you money."

> "My mother used to say the river doesn't argue. It just goes where the ground lets it. I've been trying to be less like the river and more like the ground."

## Brannoc Kettle

**Born** 535. **Novella** 65. **Game** 77. **Role** bell-founder, quarryman, Brannoc's foundry; vendor and upgrade master in the game.

Brannoc is huge, bald, and weathered, with a broken nose and a copper speaking horn because he is deaf in one ear from a casting accident. He wears a leather apron that has absorbed forty years of soot. His kettle-bird, **Pip**, steals anything shiny and hums in fourths.

He is a man who knows the cost of the Note and kept casting bells anyway, because the work was real and the alternative was to sit in a dark room and think. He is not proud of this. He is not ashamed enough to stop.

**Voice:** loud, blunt, warm. Workshop metaphors. Calls people "girl," "lad," or by their full name. He laughs like a door.

**Game role:** upgrades weapons and armour, sells humstone, and provides the practical history of the Chimeworks. His daughter **Maud** runs the Quarry Guild; he is officially retired and unofficially unable to stop interfering.

**Secret:** he made the jar that took Tam's voice, and he has never told Wren the full part he played. He told her Sable asked for it. He did not tell her he was the one who tuned it.

**Dialogue samples:**

> "A bell is a promise you make to metal. Break it and the metal remembers."

> "You want it lighter? Everything's lighter if you hit it hard enough. That's not smithing, that's philosophy."

> "Your mother was the best listener in the valley and the worst at leaving things alone. You've got her ears. Try to get her sense."

## Pell (Old Pell)

**Born** 520. **Novella** 80. **Game** 92. **Role** archivist of the Listening House.

Pell is bent, papery, and always wearing at least three coats. He has spectacles like two coins and a memory like a warehouse with a leak. He speaks in citations, parentheticals, and dry asides. He is the closest thing the valley has to a historian and the closest thing Wren has to a grandfather.

He catalogued the Harvests and filed them under "maintenance." He has spent forty years telling himself that a record is a kind of action. When Wren arrives, he discovers it is not.

**Voice:** precise, fussy, funny, allergic to drama. Uses "as the records have it" and "which is to say" as breathing.

**Game role:** hints, lore, puzzle nudges, and the game's optional "ask Pell" hint system. He is too frail to travel. He will tell the player the truth if they ask the right question, and sometimes if they ask the wrong one.

**Secret:** he has a copy of the Cantors' final sealed order, which he has never shown anyone. It suggests the Long Silence was not shame but a deliberate handover: the Cantors left the valley to the people who would have to live in it.

**Dialogue samples:**

> "The truth is a large building with many rooms. Most people want a particular room and call it the truth. Which room do you want?"

> "I have catalogued six hundred and forty distinct kinds of silence. The Hush is not one of them. That is the frightening part."

> "You are not the first person to come down that road with a jar and a bad idea. You are, however, the first in twelve years, so do try to be interesting."

## Mira Alk

**Born** 559. **Novella:** missing, presumed dead (left 593). **Would be** 41 at the novella, 53 in the game.

Mira was a Listener, a surveyor's daughter, and the best acoustician in the valley. She had a laugh like a bell with a crack in it, which Corin said was the only sound in Carrow Vale that could not be tuned. She wore her hair short, kept a rope map of every resonance in the valley, and argued with Sable for eleven years before she stopped arguing and went into the Hush.

She is not a saint. She left a ten-year-old daughter with an aunt and told her she would be back in a month. She was wrong. She knew the risk and took it anyway. The letter she leaves in Glasswood does not excuse her. It explains her, which is harder.

**Voice (letters):** warm, exact, unsentimental. She writes the way she measured: no wasted words, no self-pity, a dry joke when the sentence gets too heavy.

**Game role:** the central absence. The player can find traces of her in Glasswood, the Undersong, and the Hush. Whether she is alive, changed, or only an echo is a question the game lets the player answer differently. She never appears as a simple ghost or a damsel. If found, she is thin, funny, and terrifyingly calm, and she does not want to be saved.

**Dialogue samples (from letters):**

> "A chord, not a note. Many voices, willing. The bell must be given back, not fed."

> "I am still here. I am not sure I am still your mother. I am sure I love you. Those two things can both be true."

> "Do not give your voice to the bell. You are worth more than a foundation."

## Tam Alk

**Born** 577. **Lost** 591, aged 14. **Role** Wren's older brother; an echo in the Hush.

Tam was fourteen, loud, literal, and kind. He followed a sound into the fog because he thought someone was calling for help. He called for Wren. She was eight and let go of his hand.

In the Hush he is a boy made of grey light, half pleased and half uncertain, waiting to be told what to do. He is not a ghost with a mission. He is a shape of a call, and what he needs is not rescue but release.

**Voice:** fourteen. Eager, practical, slightly annoyed at being younger than everyone. He asks about the river.

**Game role:** a memory the player may encounter, never a quest vending machine. His release is part of Wren's arc, not the player's reward.

---

## Corin Alk

**Born** 555. **Died** 600. **Role** Wren's father; ferryman, talker, liar by omission.

Corin kept the ferry running through the bad years. He told jokes, remembered everyone's name, and told Wren that Tam had gone to the Low Water because he could not bear to say the other thing. He asked to be put in the reeds, not the bell. He liked rain on a copper roof.

He is dead before the novella begins. He appears in memory, in the town's stories, and in the game's opening as the reason Wren is still in Bellhollow.

## Ilse Vey / The Choirmaster

**Born** c. 140. **Cantor conductor**, House of the Octave. **Transformed** during the Harvests. **Dispersed** 600.

Ilse Vey was the conductor who discovered that a voice strengthens the Note. She was not a sadist. She was a woman who believed that a valley of ten thousand people was worth more than a hundred voices, and she was right about the arithmetic and wrong about everything else. She became the Choirmaster over four centuries of holding the Vault, a tall grey figure with a bell-plate face and hands like tuning prongs. It speaks in borrowed phrases because it has forgotten which voice is its own.

**Voice:** a chord of voices, half a beat out of sync. It quotes the living back at them. It is gentle, lonely, and utterly without a concept of consent.

**Game role:** gone after the novella, but its methods remain. The player finds its conductor's baton, its letters, and the Choir it left behind. A late-game choice is whether to let the Choir continue under a new conductor or end it.

## Maud Kettle

**Born** 570. **Game** 42. **Role** Brannoc's daughter; foreman and de facto leader of the Quarry Guild.

Maud is broad, quick, and permanently dusty. She wears her father's old speaking horn on a cord even though her hearing is fine. She believes the valley has been run by people who confuse secrecy with competence, and she is not entirely wrong. She wants the Quarry Door opened, studied, and made useful. She is willing to take risks with her own people but not with the town's children.

**Voice:** practical, impatient, fair. She negotiates in quantities. She has no patience for mysticism and a deep, unspoken awe of the Undersong.

**Game role:** faction leader, quest-giver, and the person who opened the door. She is not a villain. If the player sides with Sable, Maud is disappointed but keeps working with them; if the player sides with her, she is vindicated and a little frightened.

**Dialogue samples:**

> "I don't need the valley to be holy. I need it to be warm in winter and not full of holes."

> "My father says the stone remembers. Fine. I say the stone can remember while we eat."

> "You want to close the door? Then tell me how we pay for the next roof. With songs?"

## Orrin Quill

**Born** 556. **Game** 56. **Role** Nissa's father; boatwright of the Reedfolk.

Orrin is slow, warm, and watchful, with hands like rope. He whistles while he works and stops whistling when he is worried. He lost his wife Perrin to the Hush four years before the novella and has never said a word against her choice. He is the one who taught Nissa to map sound with knots.

**Game role:** Reedfolk elder, ferry-yard anchor, and a quiet source of wisdom about the Returning. He does not fight. He repairs.

---

## Perrin Quill

**Died** 596. **Role** Nissa's mother; walked into the Hush.

Perrin was a reed-reader and a storyteller. She believed the Hush was a tide and that fighting it was a kind of ill manners. Her choice to walk into it is treated by the Reedfolk as a death with dignity and by the Tenders as a scandal. Nissa has never decided which she believes.

---

## The Player

A Listener, newly arrived in Carrow Vale in the game's present. Name, pronouns, and appearance are chosen by the player. They are not the chosen one. They are one of a scattered few who can hear the undertone, and they have come because the valley is calling, or because they heard a rumour, or because they needed somewhere to go.

The game never tells the player who they were before. Dialogue choices let them be curious, blunt, kind, wary, or funny. They can disagree with Wren and still be her friend. They can side with any faction and still finish the story. The ending asks what they believe, not what they are.

## Relationships

- **Wren ↔ Sable:** grief and guilt, bound by the same bell. Not mother and daughter. Something harder: two people who did the wrong thing for understandable reasons and have to keep living in the same town.
- **Wren ↔ Nissa:** friendship built on work and jokes. Nissa is the first person who treats Wren as a person rather than a symbol. Wren is the first person who takes Nissa's maps seriously.
- **Wren ↔ Mira:** the absent measure. Wren's arc is not to find her mother but to stop organizing her life around the search.
- **Sable ↔ Brannoc:** old accomplices. They argue about the quarry because it is easier than arguing about the jars.
- **Nissa ↔ Maud:** wary respect. They agree that the Tenders keep too many secrets and disagree about what to do with the door.
- **Pell ↔ everyone:** the archivist who knows too much and says too little. His arc is to stop filing and start telling.
- **Pip ↔ dignity:** no contest.
