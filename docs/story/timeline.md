# Timeline

**Calendar:** years are counted from the Cantors' arrival in Carrow Vale (CR). The novella is set in 600 CR; the game begins in 612 CR.

---

## Deep history

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 0 | The Cantor houses arrive in Carrow Vale and begin surveying. |
| 10–180 | The Chimeworks are built: channels, chimes, shafts, and the Long Bell. The valley is tuned. |
| 180 | **The First Dissonance.** A crack opens in the Chimeworks; the Hush appears for the first time in recorded history. |
| 185 | Ilse Vey discovers that a voice given to the bell strengthens the Note. The first volunteers are sealed in humstone jars. |
| 190 | The Gift becomes a levy. |
| 195 | The levy becomes a hunt. The period is later called the **Harvests**. |
| 200 | **The Long Silence.** The Cantors stop speaking, enter the Undersong, and seal the door. |
| 201 | Bellhollow is founded by those who remained above. The Tending is established as a maintenance rite. The truth is deliberately forgotten. |
| 201–540 | The valley is stable. The Note is fed occasionally, usually by grief or misplaced honour. The Tenders become an institution. |
| 540 | The spring begins to fail. The Tenders compensate with more frequent feedings. |
| 559 | Mira Alk is born in Bellhollow. |
| 577 | Tam Alk is born. |
| 581 | Nissa Quill is born in Reedwater. |
| 583 | Wren Alk is born. |
| 586 | Mira's first descent into the Undersong and the Hush. She returns changed, with the chord. |
| 591 | Tam Alk is lost to the Hush, aged fourteen. His last call is caught in a listening jar. Sable feeds it to the Long Bell. |
| 593 | Mira Alk walks into the Hush a second time and does not return. Officially, she is dead. |
| 594 | Wren, aged eleven, is sent to her aunt in the Low Water. |
| 596 | Perrin Quill, Nissa's mother, chooses to walk into the Hush. |
| 600 | **The novella.** Corin Alk dies. Wren returns. The Long Bell cracks; the voices are released; Sable is deafened; the spring returns. |
| 601 | Repairs. Wren stays as Listener. The seasonal Chord begins. |
| 606 | The Quarry Guild reopens the deep shaft under Maud Kettle. |
| 610 | The Guild breaches the **Quarry Door**. It begins to ring on its own. |
| 612 | **The game begins.** A new Listener arrives in Carrow Vale. |

## The novella, day by day

| Day | Events |
|---|---|
| 1 | Wren descends the switchbacks, hears the thin undertone, passes the gatehouse (Tending in five days), sees the Hush, arrives in Bellhollow. Confronts Sable about the reed rite. The bell falters at midnight. |
| 2 | Reedwater: meets Nissa, clears the choke, finds the Cantor survey stone, sings the chord, scatters Corin's ashes. The Hush imitates Tam's voice. |
| 3 | Brannoc's foundry: the practice bell speaks in Tam's voice. Learns about the listening jar. Receives Mira's tuning fork and three chimes. |
| 4 | Pell's archive: the Cantors, the Harvests, the Long Silence, Mira's notebook, the Choirmaster. The tower: Sable admits feeding Tam's voice to the bell. Pell gives Wren the Glasswood map. That night: the Undersong. The Vault of Voices. The Choirmaster. Escape with a jar. |
| 5 | Before dawn: leave for Glasswood. Mira's camp and letter. Enter the Hush. Find Tam. Give him the memory of the river. Sing the chord. Return to Nissa. |
| 6 | Return to Bellhollow at dusk. The Tending has begun. Wren plays the jar, tells the truth, leads the Chord. Sable rings the giving stroke. The bell cracks; the voices are released; Sable is deafened; the spring returns at dawn. |

The six-day compression is deliberate: the Tending is a deadline, and the valley's crisis does not wait for a comfortable pace.

| 1. The Cracked Bell | 1–2 | The player arrives; meets Wren, Sable, Nissa, Brannoc, Pell; learns Listen, movement, combat; the Quarry Door rings; the Returning appear on the west road. |

| 6. What the Silence Kept | 15–17 | The Hush; the Returning; Tam's echo; Mira's hollow; the final choice is framed. |

The game's "days" are narrative markers, not a timed calendar. The player can take as long as they like; the valley waits.

## Continuity rules

1. The novella is the primary source. If a lore document conflicts with it, the lore document is wrong.
2. Wren is the novella's protagonist. The game's player is a different Listener, arriving twelve years later. The game never retells the novella as a quest; it shows its consequences.
3. The Long Bell cracked in 600 and remains cracked and silent. The tower was shored but not rebuilt. The spring runs clear.
4. Sable is deaf after 600. She communicates by lip-reading and a slate book. She is not Warden in the game, though people still call her that.
5. The Choirmaster dispersed in 600. The Choir that remains in the game is a new composite, not Ilse Vey reborn; it may use her methods and fragments of her voice.
6. Mira's status is deliberately unresolved. The game may confirm she is alive, changed, or gone; it never contradicts the letter's account of her choice.
7. The Hush is thinner after 600 but not gone. The Returning are a post-600 phenomenon.
8. No character in the game knows the full truth at the start. Pell knows the most and says the least. Sable knows the cost and not the cure. Wren knows the cure and not the cost.
