# Locations

*The valley is one connected place. Regions load at diegetic thresholds; every threshold autosaves. Distances are walking times at a normal pace, not fast-travel abstractions.*

---

## Bellhollow (hub)

A town of ochre plaster, green copper, wet stone, and hanging lanterns, built on three terraces inside the dry basin of an ancient waterfall. Population about three hundred. It smells of woodsmoke, reed dust, and the particular mineral sweetness of the spring.

**The Basin.** The town's public room: a broad stone floor where the waterfall once fell, ringed by steps. Market stalls on ordinary days; the Tending and the seasonal Chord on festival days. The acoustics are extraordinary, which is the point. A whisper at the north edge can be heard at the south if the wind is right.

**The Hollow Bell.** The stone tower at the basin's centre, shaped like a bell by a Cantor who had never seen one. Cracked from lip to shoulder since the novella, shored with quarry stone and new timber. The **Long Bell** hangs inside, split down the middle and silent. The tower is open to the public on festival days and sealed otherwise. The platform at the bell's shoulder is where Sable and Wren had their confrontation; it is now a place where the town leaves small copper tokens.

**The Third Terrace.** Houses, the blue door of the Alk family home (occupied by the Varden family), and the **Listening House**, a round cistern-archive cut into the rock. The Listening House has a new window, a public reading room, and a back door onto the old water stair.

**The Ferry Dock.** North side of the basin, where the channel leaves the town. Nissa's ferry, the *Reedwife*, is tied here. A repair scaffold, a rope walk, and a shed full of tar.

**The Foundry Road.** Switchbacks down to Brannoc's foundry at the quarry road's foot. The foundry is black stone, green copper, and permanent heat. Pip the kettle-bird patrols the roof.

**The Warden's House.** A plain slate-roofed house on the second terrace, with a slate book by the door and a drawer that Sable does not open.

**The West Road.** The only land route out of the valley. It runs along the Hush edge and is closed by a Tender gate. In the game, the gate is a quest location and a place where the Returning gather.

**The Old Sluice.** Beneath the north terrace, behind ivy trained into a bell shape. The upper entrance to the Undersong. Pell has the key; the Tenders have the other.

**The Steps.** Twelve switchbacks down from the valley rim. The game begins at the ninth, where Wren meets the player.

**The Kettle-Bird Roost.** A crumbling dovecote on the east terrace, full of kettle-birds and stolen spoons. Optional; a good place to find Pip and a small side quest.

## Reedwater (first open region)

A wide, shallow braid of channels, reed beds, floating islets, and boardwalks west of Bellhollow. The light is horizontal and silver. Herons stand in the shallows like unpaid inspectors. The water is cold, clear in the north channel, grey-brown in the south.

**The North Channel.** The valley's main artery, choked at the start of the game by dead reed, silt, and hush-mold. Clearing it is the first quest. The **ferry** crosses here; Nissa poles it by hand.

**The Ferry House.** A stilted reed-thatch house with a stove, a rope map on the wall, and too many chairs. Nissa's home. Tea is mandatory.

**The Choke.** A mass of drowned reed where the channel narrows. The **Cantor survey stone** is buried here, marked with the three-note chord. In the game it is the first puzzle clue and the first Listen-pulse tutorial.

**The Sunken Choir.** A half-drowned Cantor shrine built into a bell-mouthed stone outcrop, visible only at the right angle. The entrance is a flooded stair. Inside: a nave of black water, a sluice puzzle, an echo maze, choir stalls, and a boss arena under a ruined stained-glass window. The **Choir Chime** is kept at the altar.

**The Reed Islets.** Floating mats of root and reed, some stable enough to build on, some not. A good place for camps, songbeds, and the optional Chord Sockets puzzle.

**The Heron Bridges.** Narrow boardwalks between islets, some broken, some repaired by the Reedfolk. Falling in is safe but cold; the current pushes the player to a bank, not to death.

**The Reedfolk Boatyard.** A long shed and a mud slip where Orrin Quill builds and repairs boats. The social centre of the Reedfolk in the valley. A place for the ferry-repair side quest.

**The Kestrel Shelf.** A limestone outcrop at the northern edge of Reedwater, climbable with the hook. A cave mouth opens into a procedural Undersong side-cave: three to five rooms, one resource cluster, one of three puzzle archetypes, no story-critical items. The same seed persists per save.

**The Drowned Shrine.** A small optional shrine under the south boardwalk, reachable by swimming. Contains an Echo and a charm. The water is dark but not deep; there are no jumpscares.

## The Sunken Choir (dungeon)

**Theme:** a place built for listening that has been flooded by silence.

**Entrance Hall.** A bell-shaped chamber, half underwater, with a stone lip for walking. The water level is controlled by the sluice.

**The Nave.** A long hall of black water and pale columns. Choir stalls line the walls. The acoustics turn every footstep into a chord. Enemies: Reed Crawlers in the shallows, a Choir Moth in the loft.

**The Sluice.** Three valves and a cistern. The player routes water to lower the nave, open the inner door, and reveal a hidden Echo. Two valid routes; one leaves a shortcut open.

**The Echo Maze.** A lightless gallery where the Listen pulse reveals geometry for two seconds. Three routes; one contains a secret and an optional lore tablet. Nissa holds a lantern and comments, but does not navigate.

**The Choir Loft.** A high gallery with a broken rail and a view of the boss arena. A Choir Moth nest. An optional shortcut back to the entrance.

**The Stag's Window (boss arena).** A circular stone floor beneath a ruined stained-glass window. Three chime columns stand at the edges. The window's remaining glass shows a sequence of three figures; the chimes must be rung in that order to break the Hollow Stag's shell. The water rises and falls during the fight, changing the safe ground.

**The Altar.** Behind the arena, a small room with the Choir Chime on a stone table. The choice of where the chime goes happens after the boss, in Bellhollow.

## Kestrel Crags (designed; partially present as a shelf)

Pale limestone shelves, wind-cut arches, quarry terraces, rope bridges, and hawk nests. The sky occupies most of the frame. Grass is sparse; the palette cools with altitude.

**The Quarry Terraces.** Cut steps, spoil heaps, and the Guild's workings. Maud Kettle's office is a shack with a stove and a very good lock.

**The Sundial Stair.** A Cantor stair that aligns with the light at a specific hour of the *in-game* day. The puzzle can be solved by waiting (the game's day is 40 minutes) or by using a mirror to fake the angle. Two solutions.

**The Deep Shaft.** The Guild's great cut, descending to the **Quarry Door**: a sealed Cantor reliquary with a bell boss and a riddle in interval marks. The door is the game's inciting breach.

**The Hawk Nests.** Optional climbing routes, an Echo, and a view that reveals the valley's bell shape.

**The Wind Arches.** Natural arches that hum in different notes. A traversal puzzle and a shortcut to Glasswood.

## Glasswood (mystery region)

A petrified pine forest on the south-eastern slope: silver-grey trunks without bark, branches like frozen lightning, glassgrass that rings underfoot. Sound behaves strangely; the mix thins, footsteps return late, and the score drops out. The light is refracted into thin spectral lines.

**The Edge.** Where the glassgrass begins. A Reedfolk warning marker: three knots on a post.

**Mira's Camp.** A hollow under a stone pine: bedroll, cold fire, cracked lantern, tin box with the letter. The emotional centre of the game's middle act.

**The Listening Trees.** Hollow trunks that amplify a hum. A puzzle where the player must find the tree that carries the chord and use it to open a stone door.

**The Glass Observatory.** A Cantor instrument for watching the stars through the valley's resonance. The **Cartographer's Widow** side quest. A light-and-shadow puzzle with two solutions.

**The Hush Edge.** The western boundary, where the silver trees stop and the grey begins. A place for the Returning, a camp, and the transition to the endgame.

## The Undersong (main dungeon complex)

**Theme:** a cathedral built inside an instrument, too large for the people who now use it.

**The Upper Gallery.** The old sluice entrance; a long stair, then a walkway over black water.

**The Cathedral of Columns.** Thirty-foot ribs, hollow columns, a chord that has been holding for four hundred years. The water reflects rooms that are not there. The player can ring columns to open paths; each column is a note.

**The Vault of Voices.** A round room lined with thousands of sealed jars. The Choirmaster's hall. The game's most important lore location and its most disturbing. The jars hum; the player can read names.

**The Tuning Floor.** The original Cantor control room: a circular floor of humstone inlays, a conductor's stand, and the **First Bell**, small and never rung. The Quarry Door opens onto a collapsed section of this floor.

**The Deep Shafts.** Vertical resonant wells. A traversal challenge with wind and falling water. Optional; contains an Echo and a charm.

**The Sealed Door.** The Cantors' exit, sealed from the inside. What is behind it is a late-game reveal, not a loot room.

## The Hush (endgame region)

A grey field where sound dies. Roofless houses, walls with windows, carts without wheels, a sky with no sun. The ground is covered in small grey stones that hum faintly, each on a different note. Memory thins; the HUD minimises; captions go grey.

**The Threshold.** The last line of colour. Nissa anchors the player with a rope.

**The Stone Field.** The humming stones. Tam's echo appears here. The player gives a memory, not a trade.

**The Choirmaster's Hall.** A roofless Cantor hall, grey and vast, where the Choir gathers. The Choirmaster is smaller here, or the Hush makes it seem so.

**Mira's Hollow.** A place that might be Mira's camp, or her workshop, or her grave. The game never says for certain. The player can leave an offering, sing, or walk away.

**The Far Grey.** The deep Hush, visible but not enterable in this build. It is where the game's endings take the story.

---

## The Low Water (optional late region)

The drowned lowland west of the valley: boat wrecks, fog, reed islands, the Reedfolk winter anchorage. A place of departure and return. The game's most open exploration, with no main-story requirement. Home to the Reedfolk's oldest songs and the wreck of a Cantor ship that was never supposed to exist.

## Map and travel

- Bellhollow ↔ Reedwater: the ferry (30 seconds) or the north bank walk (2 minutes).
- Reedwater ↔ Sunken Choir: the flooded stair.
- Bellhollow ↔ Kestrel Crags: the quarry road (3 minutes).
- Bellhollow ↔ Glasswood: the south stair (4 minutes), or the wind arches shortcut.
- Bellhollow ↔ Undersong: the old sluice.
- Glasswood ↔ Hush: the threshold.
- The Low Water: ferry from the west dock, unlocked after the ferry side quest.

There is no fast travel by menu. Lanterns are save points and map reveals, not teleporters. The valley is meant to be walked and re-walked; shortcuts open permanently so that re-walking is a pleasure, not a tax.

---

## Save and rest points

- **Lanterns** (about one per sub-area) — light, map reveal, autosave, companion banter.
- **Camps** (built) — rest, heal, craft, companion conversation, manual save.
- **Region thresholds** — autosave on both sides.
- **The Listening House** — the hub save point, with a bed and Pell's tea.
